Nota ringkas Case Closed

Setiap hentian memberi murid satu kad fakta selepas lulus. Ini kad bagi unit ini:

  • clue / evidenceA clue is a piece of information that helps solve a mystery. Evidence is facts or objects that show that something is true. A witness is a person who saw something happen.
  • alibiAn alibi is proof that a person was somewhere else when a crime happened. A strong alibi can show that a suspect is innocent.
  • how sure are you?must be = I'm sure it is (100% logic). might be / could be = it is possible. can't be = I'm sure it isn't. Past: must have + past participle (sure), might have (possible), can't have (sure it didn't happen).
  • present or past?Present: modal + base verb (He must be at home). Past: modal + have + past participle (He must have gone home). Continuous: must be waiting / must have been waiting.
  • timelineA timeline lists events in the order in which they happened. Detectives use timelines to find out who could have been at a place at a particular time.
  • eliminateTo eliminate a suspect is to show that the person could not have done it, for example because they have a strong alibi.
  • how sure is the speaker?Listen for must (sure), might / could (possible) and can't (sure it's not). They tell you how certain the speaker is about a conclusion.
  • clues in orderIn a mystery story, the detective explains clues one by one. Listen for first, then, finally and for the conclusion with therefore or so.
  • suspenseSuspense is the feeling of excitement and worry that you feel when you do not know what will happen next. Mystery stories use suspense to keep readers interested.
  • inferTo infer is to understand something that is not stated directly, by using clues in the text. Phrases like must have been or might be show inference.
  • write like a detectiveSupport each deduction with a clue: The window is open, so someone must have come in. Use must, might and can't to show how sure you are.
  • watch the formPast deduction: modal + have + past participle. Do not write: must have go / can't had gone / might have went. The past participle of go is gone; of see is seen; of take is taken.

Contoh soalan

5 daripada 54 soalan dalam unit ini. Soalan lain, termasuk soalan berajah, soalan bos dan TP6, dimainkan dalam permainan.

  1. TP1

    Which word means 'facts or objects that prove that something is true'?

    1. opinion
    2. rumour
    3. fiction
    4. evidence
    Lihat jawapan

    Jawapan: D. evidence
    Evidence proves that something is true.

  2. TP1

    To work out an answer from the clues is to

    1. delay
    2. deduce
    3. decorate
    4. donate
    Lihat jawapan

    Jawapan: B. deduce
    To deduce is to reach a conclusion from evidence.

  3. TP1

    Which word means 'not guilty'?

    1. anxious
    2. reckless
    3. innocent
    4. grateful
    Lihat jawapan

    Jawapan: C. innocent
    Innocent means not guilty of a crime.

  4. TP2

    The lights are on, so somebody ____ at home.

    1. can't be
    2. might not be
    3. couldn't be
    4. must be
    Lihat jawapan

    Jawapan: D. must be
    We are sure from the evidence: must be.

  5. TP2

    She ____ the thief. She was on holiday in Sabah at the time.

    1. can't be
    2. must be
    3. might be
    4. should be
    Lihat jawapan

    Jawapan: A. can't be
    We are sure that she is not the thief: can't be.

Contoh ulasan PBD

TP1: Murid recognises a few basic words on mysteries and modals of deduction with a lot of support. Suggestion: revise the word cards in Stop 1 with a friend and use pictures to remember the meanings.

TP2: Murid understands key vocabulary on mysteries and modals of deduction but still makes basic grammar errors. Suggestion: practise short sentences using the target grammar every day.

TP3: Murid reads short texts on mysteries and modals of deduction and finds specific details accurately. Murid achieves the expected target for this unit.

TP4: Murid understands spoken English on mysteries and modals of deduction and identifies main ideas and specific details well. Murid is working towards exceeding the target.

TP5: Murid reads longer texts on mysteries and modals of deduction, makes good inferences and guesses the meaning of unfamiliar words from context. Very good progress.

TP6: Murid uses English accurately and independently on mysteries and modals of deduction, corrects errors and builds well-organised sentences. Murid exceeds the target and is an exemplary model to others.
